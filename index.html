About

I'm a PhD student in Computer Science at Northwestern University, advised by Matthew Groh in the Human-AI Collaboration Lab. I'm also affiliated with the Northwestern Institute on Complex Systems (NICO) and the Ryan Institute on Complexity.

My current project asks whether a computational approach can evaluate voice similarity well enough to match, or even exceed, the "reasonable person" standard. That means designing the whole experimental workflow, from a behavioral study of how people perceive voice identity, to controlled speech samples produced with an adapted voice generation model, to a contrastive classifier that models how machines perceive voice identity.

Before Northwestern, I completed a master's degree in data science at the University of Wisconsin-Madison, where I worked with Claudia Solís-Lemus on neural networks for phylogenetic inference and on Bayesian deep learning for soil microbiome data.

In short, I research things machines struggle to define, humans barely agree on, and funding agencies find suspiciously unprofitable.

  • Symmetry-preserving neural networks for phylogenetic inference

    A neural network that infers phylogenetic trees from sequence data and builds the symmetries of the tree into its architecture. It outperformed standard phylogenetic methods and earlier neural networks under long branch attraction, and it uses less memory. Published in Bioinformatics Advances.

  • Predicting potato yield and disease from the soil microbiome

    Random forest and Bayesian neural network models that predict potato yield and disease from soil microbiome data together with chemical and physical soil properties. The study shows how much the preprocessing choices people make change predictive performance. Published in BMC Bioinformatics.
